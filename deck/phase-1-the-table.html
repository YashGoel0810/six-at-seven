01 — The problem

Founders are surrounded by people
and alone anyway.

The founder's job structurally removes every honest audience. This is not a soft problem — it is the reason the existing options do not work.

Who they cannot say it to
  • The team. Admitting fear to people whose salaries depend on you is a leadership failure.
  • Investors. Every conversation is implicitly a fundraise. You perform.
  • Friends and family. They cannot calibrate. "That sounds stressful" is not help.
  • Other founders at events. Everyone is selling. Nobody says the real number.
Why the existing answers fail

Conferences optimise for volume of contact. You meet forty people and remember none. The format actively prevents depth.

WhatsApp and Slack groups are free, so nobody is filtered, and nobody is accountable. They decay into link-dumping within a quarter.

Paid communities sell access to a directory. A directory is not a relationship.

The gap: a room small enough that you cannot hide, with people vetted enough that honesty is safe.

This deck solves the isolation problem. The team-formation problem is Phase 2.01
02 — Why now

The behaviour is already proven.
Just not here, and not for founders.

This is not a bet on whether people will pay to eat with strangers. That question has been answered at scale. The open question is whether a founder-specific version retains.

Proof of demand
€18M

Timeleft ARR, 20 months after launching weekly dinners between strangers. 150,000 diners a month, 200+ cities, ~100 staff.

Proof it starts small
€125

Timeleft's first week of revenue — a Typeform, a WhatsApp group and a Stripe link. No app. They ran to €1M/month on no-code.

The Indian market
2.23L

DPIIT-recognised startups in India; 55,200 added in FY26 alone, up 51.6% year on year. Delhi NCR is a top-three cluster.

The insight worth stealing: Timeleft treats the dinner itself as the technology — a table, five strangers, three hours, and the natural structure of a meal. No hosts, no ambassadors, no icebreakers. That is why it scales without a local team in every city, and it is the operating model this phase copies.

03 — The product

Six founders. One table.
Thursday, 7pm.

Matched by questionnaire, verified by a human, seated at venues we have personally eaten in. The constraints are the product.

Seat 01–02

Roughly your altitude. Pre-revenue with pre-revenue, post-Series-A with post-Series-A. Advice only lands between people carrying similar weight.

Seat 03

Someone further along — a founder who has already been where the table is heading. Not a mentor, not billing you.

Seat 04

Someone who disagrees with you. The questionnaire measures how you handle being wrong, and we seat against you deliberately.

Seat 05–06

Nobody from your circle. Same cap table, same accelerator batch, same phone contacts — all filtered out.

The three rules
  • No pitching. Nobody is seated to sell to anyone.
  • No recording, no posting, no adding people to a CRM afterwards.
  • No investors in disguise. A fund that wants in hosts its own table, announced up front.
Why the rules are the moat

Anyone can copy a dinner. What is hard to copy is a room where the no-pitch rule is actually enforced, because enforcement costs you revenue in the short term — you must reject paying applicants who would sell into the room.

That short-term cost is exactly why most competitors will not hold the line, and why the rule compounds into reputation.

Guests settle the bill with the venue directly — food never enters our P&L03
04 — The formats

Five formats, one roster.

Timeleft's members went from 1.4 to 2.2 experiences a month when formats were added beyond dinner. More formats is the single proven lever on retention and revenue per member.

FormatCadenceSizeWhoPurpose
The TableWeekly, Thu 7pm6MembersThe core product. Everything else assumes this works.
The Post-MortemMonthly8–10Members + 1 guestOne founder walks through a failure in detail. Most bonding, most shareable.
The WorkbenchFortnightly4–6MembersWorking session on one live problem. Shifts the product from company to usefulness.
Long TableQuarterly30–40Public, ticketedAcquisition engine and public face. The room Phase 2 recruits from.
Off-MenuOpportunistic6–8Members onlyA specific operator hosts — an investor, a second-time founder. Scarcity drives renewals.

Sequencing rule: launch with The Table only. Add one format per quarter, and only after the prior one clears 50 attendees a month. Timeleft opened 325 cities in a year and had to retreat to 220 — depth before breadth is their own stated correction.

Q1 — prove one format

The Table, weekly. Nothing else exists. The only metric that counts is whether people come back.

Q2 — add depth

The Post-Mortem. Same roster, different register, and the format most likely to travel as content.

Q3 — add utility

The Workbench. Justifies a renewal in month nine, when novelty has worn off.

Q4 — add reach

Long Table. The public face and the acquisition engine.

One format at a time. Adding formats before the first retains is how this fails.04
05 — Unit economics

₹899 a month. 83% contribution margin.

The structural choice that makes this work: the guest pays the restaurant directly. Food never touches our P&L — no inventory risk, no minimum spend, no working capital on covers.

One part-time ops person, tools, venue scouting. Deliberately minimal — no app, no full-time hires until the format is proven.

Breakeven: ~70 paying members. At ₹899 that is ₹62,930 of monthly revenue against ₹55,000 of fixed cost plus variable.

Why margin is not the risk

Asset-light by construction, so gross margin is high almost regardless of execution.

The risks that decide this business are churn and CAC, and both are currently unmeasured assumptions. Slide 12 handles them.

An early version of this model showed 72x LTV:CAC. It was discarded — it costed only the marginal seat and ignored fixed operations entirely.

Why ₹899

Below ₹1,000 clears the threshold where a founder expenses it without thinking. Above ₹500 it filters out people who will not show up. Priced to be forgotten, not negotiated.

Why not per-dinner pricing

Ticket pricing makes every month a fresh purchase decision and caps revenue at attendance. Membership charges for access and lets us add formats without repricing.

The LTV question

At 10% monthly churn, average tenure is 10 months and LTV is ~₹7,490. Every acquisition decision follows from that number, and that number follows from churn we have not yet measured.

Venue assumption: Delhi NCR group dining sourced at ₹900–1,100 + tax per head, settled by the guest05

07 — Who this is for

Narrow on purpose. The filter is the product.

Every person admitted who should not be there degrades the experience for five others. Admission criteria are therefore commercial decisions, not administrative ones.

Primary — admit
  • Delhi NCR, 24–38, pre-seed to Series A
  • Running something real: registered entity, live product, or revenue
  • Full-time on it — this is their job, not a side project
  • Has a team they cannot be fully honest with

Seed every table with one second-time founder, seated free. They raise the quality of the room and are the strongest single retention lever for the other five.

Explicitly — reject
  • Aspiring founders and students. Different problem, different room.
  • Service businesses looking for clients. The room is a lead list to them.
  • Anyone whose stated goal involves selling into the table.
  • Investors attending as investors, unless hosting openly.

Rejection is revenue you turn down. That is the cost of the product being real, and it is the line most competitors will not hold.

Market size, honestly stated

2.23L DPIIT-recognised startups nationally; NCR is a top-three cluster. Even a narrow read — full-time founders, right stage, right city, willing to pay — leaves a serviceable pool in the tens of thousands.

At 650 members, month 24, we would hold a low single-digit percentage of that pool. The constraint is not market size. It is retention.

Verification: government ID plus one proof of building — domain, GST number, or app listing. A human checks both.07
08 — Go to market

Hand-built to 100. Paid after.

The first hundred members cannot be bought, because the product is who is in the room and paid acquisition cannot control that.

Stage 1 · Months 0–3 · to 100
  • Hand-pick and personally invite the first 60 from existing networks
  • Seed every table with one second-time founder, free
  • Zero ad spend — referral and invitation only
  • Typeform + WhatsApp + UPI. No app, no build

Goal: 12 tables run, and a measured month-3 return rate.

Stage 2 · Months 4–9 · to 400
  • Instagram and LinkedIn creative built from real table footage
  • Founder-operator content — the post-mortem format is inherently shareable
  • Referral mechanic: a member who brings one pays nothing that month
  • Venue co-marketing: restaurants promote their own slow weeknights

Goal: CAC under ₹300, churn under 10%.

Stage 3 · Months 9–24
  • Long Table becomes the public showcase and VC touchpoint
  • Membership tiers tested — annual prepay at a discount
  • Corporate/accelerator partnerships for bulk memberships
  • Only then consider a second city

Goal: 650 members, profitable, roster ready for Phase 2.

Channel truth

Instagram carries the reach in Indian metros, but LinkedIn carries the credibility for a founder-gated product. Instagram for fill, LinkedIn for who fills it.

The creative that works

Not the food. The empty sixth chair, the rules, and one real post-mortem told plainly. Sell the constraint, never the restaurant.

Referral beats spend

A member who brings one pays nothing that month — ₹899 of margin for a pre-vetted member at roughly a third of paid CAC.

Channel priority follows Timeleft's own sequence: prove the format manually, then open with ads, then add formats for depth08
09 — Competition

Nobody owns founder-specific, paid, in-person in India.

The adjacent players are each strong at something and structurally unable to do this. That gap is the opening — but it is an opening, not an empty field.

The honest risk

Timeleft launched Mumbai and Bengaluru in 2025 with €18M ARR behind them. Treat NCR as contested, not empty. If they go vertical on founders before we have a roster, the window narrows sharply.

Not the format — that is copyable in a weekend. The defensible assets are the vetted roster, the enforcement reputation, and the behavioural data on how members collaborate. All three compound, and all three are what Phase 2 is built from.

10 — What kills this

Three risks. One of them is existential.

Listed in order of how likely they are to end the business, not in order of comfort.

1 — Churn after month 3
Existential

Novelty fades. If month-4 retention is weak, nothing else in this deck works — the model, the GTM, and all of Phase 2 assume a roster that stays.

Mitigation: add formats (proven +57% experience frequency at Timeleft). Measure cohort retention before spending a rupee on ads.

Stop rule: churn above 12% in any month halts all acquisition spend until it is fixed.

2 — One bad actor in a room

The no-pitch rule is the product. A single person selling into a table damages five memberships at once, and the damage is not recoverable by refund.

Mitigation: human review of every table before it goes out. ID plus proof-of-building. Breaking the rule ends the membership immediately, with no exceptions made for revenue.

3 — Founder-dependency

Early curation quality is personal taste, and personal taste does not obviously scale to a second city or a hired matcher.

Mitigation: document the matching rubric from table one. Treat curation as a written system that someone else could run, not as judgement. If it cannot be written down by month 12, a second city is off the table.

CAC above ₹500 or churn above 12% both trigger the same response: stop spending, fix retention10
11 — Where this leads

Phase 1 is a real business.
It is also the only way to earn Phase 2.

The events business stands on its own. But it produces three assets that cannot be bought, and those assets are what make a co-founder formation product possible at all.

01
A vetted roster

Hundreds of founders, ID-verified, proof-of-building checked, already paying. Phase 2 recruits from this pool instead of buying strangers.

02
Behavioural data

We have watched these people argue, disagree, handle being wrong, and show up or not. No questionnaire produces that, and it is exactly what co-founder matching needs.

03
Earned trust

People who have had eight good dinners with us will pay ₹25,000 for a six-week sprint. Cold traffic will not.

The gate: Phase 2 does not launch until Phase 1 shows a stable month-6 cohort. Launching formation on a roster that does not retain means recruiting strangers, which is precisely the model that killed Lunchclub — 300,000 users, 5 million introductions, $24M raised, free, and shut down in 2022. That argument is made in full in the Phase 2 deck.

12 — First 90 days

Run one table before building anything.

No app, no brand system, no second city, no backend. Typeform, WhatsApp, UPI, six people, one restaurant.

Weeks 1–2
  • Write the questionnaire and the matching rubric
  • Walk five venues, eat at three, pick one
  • Hand-pick the first 12 names
Weeks 3–6
  • Run tables 1 and 2, two weeks apart
  • Sit at both. Take notes on what breaks
  • Call every attendee within 48 hours
Weeks 7–12
  • Run tables 3–8, weekly
  • Introduce the ₹899 membership at table 4
  • Measure the month-3 return rate
The only number that matters

How many of the six ask, unprompted, when the next one is.

If it is four or more, the model in this deck is worth acting on and the ₹3–4L is worth committing. If it is one or two, the format is wrong and no amount of spend fixes it. That single measurement, at a cost of roughly ₹15,000 and six weeks, decides everything else here.
