# Strategy — The Table, then The Build

Two products, deliberately sequenced. Phase 1 is a small-format events business
that generates cash from month one. Phase 2 is a co-founder formation product
that is only possible because Phase 1 already earned the trust and the roster.

Everything below is either sourced from a named comparable or computed from
stated assumptions. Nothing here is a measured result of our own — we have not
run a table yet. Assumptions are flagged as such throughout.

---

## 1. The strategic argument

**Phase 1 without Phase 2 is a lifestyle business.** Dinner clubs are real
businesses — Timeleft reached roughly €18M ARR on this exact model — but they
are capped by how many rooms you can fill, and they have no defensible moat. A
competitor can copy the format in a weekend.

**Phase 2 without Phase 1 is a graveyard.** Lunchclub had 300,000 users, made
five million introductions, raised $24M from a16z, and shut down in 2022. It was
free, it worked, and it still died. Bumble Bizz was discontinued in most
regions. The pattern is consistent: **matching people is not, by itself, a
business.** The value is real but too diffuse for the matched party to pay for
directly.

**The sequence is the strategy.** Phase 1 produces three assets that Phase 2
cannot buy: a vetted roster of founders, observed behavioural data on how they
argue and collaborate, and enough trust that people will pay for something
higher-stakes. Phase 2 monetises those assets through a cohort fee and
deal-flow partnerships — not through a matching subscription.

## 2. Phase 1 — The Table

### 2.1 What it is

Curated dinners of six founders, Thursdays, Delhi NCR. Matched by a
questionnaire, verified by a human, seated at venues we have personally eaten
in. No pitching, no recording, no adding people to a CRM afterwards.

### 2.2 The five formats

Launch with **one**. Add the next only when the current one clears 50 attendees
a month. Timeleft opened 325 cities in a year and had to retreat to about 220 —
depth before breadth is their own stated correction, learned expensively.

| Format | Cadence | Size | Who | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Table** | Weekly, Thu 7pm | 6 | Members | The core product. Everything else is built on this working. |
| **The Post-Mortem** | Monthly | 8–10 | Members + 1 guest | One founder walks through a failure in detail. The most shareable format, and the most bonding. |
| **The Workbench** | Fortnightly | 4–6 | Members | Working session on one person's live problem. Shifts the product from *company* to *usefulness* — this is what justifies a renewal in month nine. |
| **Long Table** | Quarterly | 30–40 | Public, ticketed | Acquisition engine and public face. The room Phase 2 recruits from. |
| **Off-Menu** | Opportunistic | 6–8 | Members only | A specific operator hosts — an investor, a second-time founder. Scarcity drives renewals. |

**Sequencing:** Q1 The Table only. Q2 add Post-Mortem. Q3 add Workbench.
Q4 add Long Table. Off-Menu whenever a host volunteers.

Timeleft's members went from 1.4 to 2.2 experiences a month once formats were
added beyond dinner. More formats is the single proven lever on both retention
and revenue per member — but only after the first one retains.

### 2.3 Unit economics

**Structural choice: the guest pays the restaurant directly.** This is how
Timeleft operates and it is the reason the model is asset-light — food never
touches our P&L, so we carry no inventory risk, no minimum spend, no working
capital on covers.

Assumptions (Delhi NCR group dining sourced at ₹900–1,100 + tax per head):

| Line | Value |
|---|---|
| Membership | ₹899/month |
| Dinner ticket | ₹0 to us (guest settles with venue) |
| Variable cost per member-month | ~₹150 (matching ops, payment fees, comms) |
| Contribution margin | ~83% |
| Fixed cost, months 0–6 | ~₹55,000/month (one part-time ops person, tools, venue scouting) |
| **Breakeven** | **~70 paying members** |
| Capital required to month 6 | ₹3–4L |
| Year-2 EBITDA, NCR only | ~₹1.04Cr |

**Why margin is not the risk.** The model is asset-light by construction, so
gross margin is high almost regardless of execution. The two risks that
actually matter are **churn** and **CAC**, and both are currently unmeasured
assumptions. An early model I built showed 72x LTV:CAC — I discarded it,
because it costed only the marginal seat and ignored fixed operations
entirely. Treat any LTV:CAC above ~5x in this category as a modelling error
until real cohort data exists.

**The stop rule.** If CAC crosses ₹500 or monthly churn crosses 12%, stop all
spending and fix retention before scaling. Scaling a leaky bucket is the
standard way this category dies.

### 2.4 Target group

**Primary:** Delhi NCR founders, 24–38, pre-seed to Series A, running something
real — a registered entity, a live product, or revenue. Isolated in a specific
way: they have a team they cannot be honest with, investors they must perform
for, and friends who do not understand the job.

**Explicitly not:** aspiring founders, students, service-business owners
looking for clients, or anyone whose primary goal is to sell into the room.
The no-pitch rule is the product, and admitting one pitcher damages five other
members' experience.

**Seed strategy:** every table gets one second-time founder, seated free. They
raise the quality of the room, and their presence is the single most effective
retention lever for the other five.

### 2.5 Go to market

**Stage 1 — months 0–3, to 100 members.** Hand-pick and personally invite the
first 60 from existing networks. Zero ad spend. Referral and invitation only.
Run on Typeform + WhatsApp + UPI — no app, no build. *Goal: 12 tables run and a
measured month-3 return rate.*

**Stage 2 — months 4–9, to 400 members.** Instagram and LinkedIn creative built
from real table footage. Founder-operator content, with the post-mortem format
as the shareable asset. Referral mechanic: a member who brings one pays nothing
that month — ₹899 of margin for a pre-vetted member at roughly a third of paid
CAC. Venue co-marketing: restaurants promote their own slow weeknights.
*Goal: CAC under ₹300, churn under 10%.*

**Stage 3 — months 9–24.** Phase 2 launches, recruited entirely from the
existing roster. Long Table becomes the public showcase and VC touchpoint. Sign
three deal-flow partners before running cohort two. Only then consider a second
city.

**Channel truth:** Instagram carries reach in Indian metros; LinkedIn carries
credibility for a founder-gated product. Instagram for fill, LinkedIn for who
fills it. The creative that works is not the food — it is the empty sixth
chair, the rules, and one real post-mortem told plainly. Sell the constraint,
never the restaurant.

## 3. Phase 2 — The Build

### 3.1 What it is

A six-week structured sprint in which pre-vetted founders test working
relationships before committing to a company together. Not a matching service —
a **formation** service. Recruited exclusively from the Phase 1 roster, where we
have already watched these people argue, disagree, and handle being wrong.

### 3.2 Why it is priced as insurance

Co-founder conflict is among the most-cited causes of startup failure, and
roughly 65% of failures are attributable to people problems rather than
product or market. ₹25,000 for six weeks is roughly a tenth of what the wrong
co-founder costs in wasted months. It is priced as insurance against a known,
expensive failure mode — not as an event ticket.

This is also why it cannot be a subscription. Nobody renews a co-founder
search monthly; they either form a company or they stop. Cohort pricing matches
how the need actually behaves.

### 3.3 Revenue lines

| Line | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Formation sprint** | ₹6.22L contribution per cohort | 40 founders × ₹25,000, less ₹3.78L venue/ops/curation over six weeks. 62% margin. Four cohorts a year → ₹24.9L. |
| **B — Deal-flow partners** | ₹18L/year | 6 VC or corporate partners at ₹3L each, for structured access to cohort demo sessions and the annual Long Table. |
| **C — Equity (optional)** | 3–5% | In teams that formally form through a sprint. ~₹1.1Cr/year expected value at 24 teams and an 8% hit rate against ₹20Cr outcomes — realised a decade later. **Treat as upside, never as budget.** |

**Line B is the one Lunchclub never built.** Funds have dedicated budget and an
attributable need — they are paying for a diligence shortcut, access to teams
they would otherwise meet six months later with formation work already visible.
That is a fundamentally easier sale than charging a founder to meet people.

**Why the cash lines come first:** A and B bill inside the quarter they are
earned. A business that needs Line C to work is a fund, not a company.

### 3.4 Target group

Phase 1 members who have signalled they are looking for a co-founder, plus a
small number of externally-referred technical founders to balance each cohort's
skill mix. Every participant is already ID-verified and behaviourally observed
through Phase 1 — which is the entire reason this product can charge what it
charges.

## 4. Risk register

| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Churn after month 3** | The single existential risk. Novelty fades; if month-4 retention is weak, nothing else in this plan works. | Add formats (proven +57% experience frequency at Timeleft). Measure cohort retention before any paid spend. |
| **Phase 1 fails to retain → Phase 2 has no roster** | Phase 2's entire premise is a vetted, observed roster. | Do not launch Phase 2 until Phase 1 shows a stable month-6 cohort. |
| **Timeleft enters NCR properly** | They launched Mumbai and Bengaluru in 2025 with €18M ARR behind them. | They serve the general population. Founder vetting and a formation product are not features they can bolt on without breaking their core model. |
| **One bad actor in a room** | The no-pitch rule is the product; a single violation damages five memberships. | Human review of every table. ID + proof-of-building. Breaking the rule ends the membership. |
| **Founder-dependency** | Early curation quality is personal and does not obviously scale. | Document the matching rubric from table one. Treat curation as a written system, not taste. |

---

## 5. What to do first

Run **one table** before building anything. No app, no brand system, no second
city. Typeform, WhatsApp, UPI, six people, one restaurant.

The only number that matters from it: how many of the six ask when the next one
is, unprompted. That single measurement determines whether any of the rest of
this document is worth acting on.
